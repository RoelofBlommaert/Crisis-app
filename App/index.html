    About this app

    Built as a learning exercise for a vibe-coding course, using a scenario relevant to MFA work: monitoring instability abroad and how it may relate to travel/tourism patterns. See Documentation/Relevance and function.txt in the repo for the full writeup, and Scripts/README.md for known data-source limitations. In short:

    • This is a one-time static snapshot, not a live feed — see the generation time above.
    • GDELT tone/volume is a media-attention proxy, not ground-truth instability.
    • GDACS covers natural disasters only; conflict-driven countries (Ukraine, Lebanon, Israel, Sudan) have no confirmed-event coverage from it.
    • CBS travel figures are region-level (not per-country) for most tracked countries — shown with a badge, not disguised as country-specific.
    • No personal, internal, or classified MFA data is used anywhere in this app.

    Conflict & disaster signals

    Each country gets two 0–100 gauges, not a statistical probability of war or disaster — there is no calibrated base rate or validated model behind them. They're scored one day at a time (see the 7-day strip under each gauge) rather than as one flat weekly number, built from:

    • Conflict signal: that day's share of articles carrying a conflict/war/protest/terrorism theme tag (via GDELT's GKG theme tagging), plus that day's tone severity and a volume-spike bonus.
    • Disaster signal: any GDACS alert level for an event actually active on that day (by the event's own date range, weighted highest), plus that day's share of articles carrying a natural-disaster theme tag.

    Scoring day-by-day (rather than "is there any matching event at all") is a deliberate fix: GDACS keeps recently-modified events listed for a while after they're actually over, so a months-old cyclone or earthquake would otherwise keep inflating "today's" score forever. Those older events still show up in the country's event list, just labelled historical instead of active. Note also that GDELT tags an article with every country it mentions, so a bordering or mediating country (e.g. Egypt or Turkey in Gaza-related coverage) can pick up a real conflict-theme share without being a combatant itself — a known limitation, not a bug we can fully resolve without deeper text analysis.
