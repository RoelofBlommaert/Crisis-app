    About this app

    This is a proof of concept, not a finished tool: it shows what public data (ACLED, GDELT, GDACS) is available and how it could combine into a per-country risk indication for a crisis coordinator — it is not an automatic, validated classifier, and it does not forecast or predict crises. See Documentation/Relevance and function.txt for the full writeup and Scripts/README.md for data-source details.

    Data sources & freshness

      ACLED: real conflict data, no account needed

      Conflict data comes from ACLED (Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project) — real monthly political-violence event and fatality counts per country, not a media-mention proxy. ACLED's own API requires registering an account; this app instead uses ACLED's own pre-aggregated country files published through the Humanitarian Data Exchange (data.humdata.org), which are freely downloadable with no login. The tradeoff is resolution: monthly, not daily or event-level. The single most recent available month is always excluded from scoring as provisional — ACLED's newest month is consistently far below the surrounding trend for nearly every country tracked here, a reporting/verification lag, not real de-escalation (visible as a sudden drop at the right edge of each ACLED chart). Per ACLED's Terms of Use, their data must be clearly attributed wherever shown — see the attribution line under each country's ACLED chart.

      Conflict & disaster signals

      Each country gets two 0–100 gauges, not a statistical probability of war or disaster — there is no calibrated base rate or validated model behind them. They're scored one day at a time (see the strip under each gauge) rather than as one flat weekly number, built from:

      • Conflict signal: a baseline severity score (real ACLED fatalities that month, log-scaled against a fixed reference of 5,000 fatalities/month — roughly the world's most severe active conflicts) plus an escalation bonus only when that month runs above the country's own trailing 12-month average — a below-baseline month gets zero bonus, never a penalty. GDELT's conflict-theme share and tone add a small corroborating nudge on top. Without any confirmed ACLED fatalities, GDELT alone cannot push this above ~10.
      • Disaster signal: any GDACS alert level for an event actually active on that day (by the event's own date range, weighted highest), plus that day's (damped) share of articles carrying a natural-disaster theme tag.

      This replaced an earlier version that banded conflict_signal directly off that month's fatality count: Ukraine (4008 fatalities), Sudan (876), and Haiti (103) all landed in the same top tier and clipped at 100 — indistinguishable, even though two of them were actually running below their own recent average that month (a stable, chronic war, not a fresh spike), while Egypt's real 3.3x jump above its own (much smaller) baseline was invisible next to them. Baseline and escalation are now shown as separate numbers (see each country's "Baseline & escalation" box) rather than collapsed into one score that loses which country is actually changing right now.

      Scoring day-by-day (rather than "is there any matching event at all") fixes a separate, earlier bug: GDACS keeps recently-modified events listed for a while after they're actually over, so a months-old cyclone or earthquake would otherwise keep inflating "today's" score forever. Those older events still show up in the country's event list, just labelled historical instead of active. Note also that GDELT tags an article with every country it mentions, so a bordering or mediating country (e.g. Egypt or Turkey in Gaza-related coverage) can pick up a real conflict-theme share without being a combatant itself — a known limitation, not something fixable without deeper text analysis.

      What's deliberately not here yet

      Per this PoC's scope: no manual override control for the coordinator to confirm or correct a suggested classification (the "automatic detection + manual override" track) — a known, deliberately deferred gap, not an oversight. No live/scheduled refresh (this is a one-time static snapshot; see the timestamp above). No real consular-call integration and no RNI linkage.
