    About this app

    Built as a learning exercise for a vibe-coding course, using a scenario relevant to MFA work: monitoring instability abroad and how it may relate to travel/tourism patterns. See Documentation/Relevance and function.txt in the repo for the full writeup, and Scripts/README.md for known data-source limitations. In short:

    • This is a one-time static snapshot, not a live feed — see the generation time above.
    • GDELT tone/volume is a media-attention proxy, not ground-truth instability.
    • GDACS covers natural disasters only; conflict-driven countries (Ukraine, Lebanon, Israel, Sudan) have no confirmed-event coverage from it.
    • CBS travel figures are region-level (not per-country) for most tracked countries — shown with a badge, not disguised as country-specific.
    • No personal, internal, or classified MFA data is used anywhere in this app.

    Conflict & disaster signals

    Each country gets two 0–100 gauges, not a statistical probability of war or disaster — there is no calibrated base rate or validated model behind them. They're a transparent heuristic built from:

    • Conflict signal: share of the last 24h's articles carrying a conflict/war/protest/terrorism theme tag (via GDELT's GKG theme tagging), plus recent tone severity and a volume-spike bonus.
    • Disaster signal: any confirmed GDACS alert level (weighted highest), plus the share of recent articles carrying a natural-disaster theme tag.

    This replaced an earlier version that color-coded countries by their flat 7-day-average tone — which stayed "Green" for Israel and Lebanon even during heavy negative coverage, since a week-long average dilutes acute events with routine reporting. Note also that GDELT tags an article with every country it mentions, so a bordering or mediating country (e.g. Egypt or Turkey in Gaza-related coverage) can pick up a real conflict-theme share without being a combatant itself — a known limitation, not a bug we can fully resolve without deeper text analysis.
